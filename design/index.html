Taiwan Waves: two retro directions

Riso almanac is the lead direction; Tide calendar shares the same pages and layout. The app itself is untouched. On spot and buoy pages, hover the timeline and the readout, compass rose and map follow along. The map has a key and three swell styles to compare (top right of the map).

Riso almanac

1.4m

A Taiwanese farmer's almanac run off a risograph in an 1980s print shop. Newsprint, federal-blue ink for everything typeset, a pink second drum for accents, and a little misregistration. Chinese leads: the spot's characters are the headline and the compass reads 北 東 南 西. The most local and graphic option.

Newsprint, blue drum, pink drum, marigold, sea tint

Tide calendar

1.4m

The free tide calendar a 1970s surf shop hands out, printed on kraft card. Chocolate ink, a four-band avocado, gold, sienna and chocolate stripe under the masthead, soft fat serif numerals (Fraunces at full softness), and a faded-teal sea on the map. The warmer, more "surf shop" option.

Paper, ink, stripe, sea
HomeSpot: JinzunBuoy: Guishandao

Round two changes

  • No layout shift. The spot readout has fixed rows and only its text changes, so scrubbing or scrolling never moves the page. Measured: zero layout-shift entries across scrolling and a 30-position sweep.
  • Wind and swell look different, not just coloured differently. Swell is always coloured and wide. Wind is always a thin ink line with an open head and knot barbs. The compass and the map use the same glyphs.
  • Map key and labels. A key in the map corner shows each glyph with its current numbers, and every arrow is labeled on the map.
  • Three swell styles to compare (your choice is remembered): Arrows, Crests, and Swell lines, which adds wind streaks across the whole map.

Simplified readout

  • Forecast and measured side by side: the MFWAM model number next to the nearest buoy's, each labeled with its source. The page ends with a plain explanation of the difference.
  • Rows: swell, 2nd swell, wind (offshore, cross-shore or onshore), tide with the next high and low, and water temperature (from the buoy, or the model when there's no buoy).
  • Wind waves removed from the readout, chart, compass and map. They're still inside the total-sea area, which is now labeled "includes local chop".
  • When the swell can't reach the beach (Baisha, for example), a warning replaces the empty line and the blocked arrows fade.
